# L-165 Mass Craft: craft a recipe many times at once

Kind: Design, then Research (strong model).
Status: Open, not chosen for building; needs the purpose-and-scope conversation a larger feature starts with. No release.

Turn a whole inventory of ingredients into products with one action (logs
into planks, iron into blocks), instead of repeating fill-the-grid and
take-the-output by hand.

## Decided
- Ctrl + click on a recipe in the recipe book crafts all, no count in the
  first version, default off: Ctrl + click lies close to Shift + click and an
  accidental mass craft would hurt (maintainer 2026-10-12).
- Crafting from nearby chests is out.

## Open
- Whether a "craft the last recipe again" key is wanted (agent: not in the
  first version).
- Agent's proposal for the rest (not decided):
  - Repeat vanilla's own steps (`CraftingScreenController::_handleRecipeSelect`,
    then `_handleCraftItem(ItemCraftType::All)`) until the ingredients run
    out, the output would not fit or the player stops. Lamium never builds
    crafting transactions; the server validates every round.
  - One round at a time: the next starts only after authoritative state shows
    ingredients down and output up; a mismatch or timeout stops with a toast.
    A per-tick bound keeps the rate modest.
  - Stops on screen close, any player click or key, focus loss, world or
    dimension change. Never drops items: check room before each round.
  - The 2x2 inventory grid and the crafting table first; stonecutter, loom,
    smithing and furnaces later or out.
  - A progress toast ("Crafting 320/640") and the total at the end.
- Watch out for: leftovers (buckets, bottles), recipes accepting several
  ingredient kinds (any log), the OreUI recipe book versus the JSON UI screen
  (L-130 found only the latter covered), servers limiting request rates
  (help-text warning like Auto Attack's).

## Next
Measure the vanilla baseline first: what one recipe-book click and one
shift-take of the output already do (crafts per action, where the output
goes, what happens when it does not fit). Craft requests already carry a
count (`ItemStackRequestActionCraftBase::mNumCrafts`), so vanilla may do more
per action than it looks.
